# Biography, summary and personal interests

## intro
My education brings together physics, statistics and computer science, through a path in which an interest in forms has extended to data, algorithms and classical and quantum information theory. Alongside research and teaching, I practise yoga and climbing, where geometry becomes a bodily experience.

## biography
### Physics, geometry and aesthetic sensibility {#fisica-geometria}

I studied Physics at the University of Milano-Bicocca, writing my bachelor’s and master’s theses under the supervision of Professor Giorgio Benedek, with Professor Marco Bernasconi and Dr Ottorino Ori as co-supervisors for the master’s thesis. From the dynamics and thermodynamics of schwarzites, negatively curved carbon structures, I moved towards questions about the relationship between atomic bond arrangements and the properties of matter, approaching topology as a way of understanding the stability and vibrations of these networks.

Benedek passed on to me an aesthetic sensibility towards scientific disciplines that profoundly shaped how I see them: in carbon nanostructures, the elegance of forms invited investigation of their physical basis, while a symmetry or a curvature suggested relationships to explore through mathematics. This experience gave rise to an interest in geometry that continues to accompany me beyond research.

### The doctorate: understanding matter {#dottorato}

My PhD in Physics and Nanosciences at the University of Modena and Reggio Emilia allowed me to explore the optical and vibrational properties of graphene nanostructures, supervised by Professor Alice Ruini, with Dr Deborah Prezzi as co-supervisor, whose patience and care contributed greatly to my development as a researcher.

Studying how changes in the shape, edges or relative arrangement of these structures affect their vibrations and response to light made the relationship between geometric description and material behaviour more tangible. Working with them gave me the time and attention needed to understand the difficulties of this research, with the human relationship providing essential support for the scientific work.

### Statistics and data analysis {#statistica}

After working in optoelectronics, I completed a second master’s degree in Data Science and Economics at the University of Milan, under the supervision of Professor Silvia Salini. Her guidance instilled a passion for statistics and data analysis, directing my attention towards the questions that precede the choice of a method: how observations are collected, which assumptions support an inference and how much confidence we can place in a conclusion. I devoted my thesis to the epistemological foundations of data analysis, bringing this education into dialogue with my background in physics.

### Learning and information theory {#fondamenti-ia}

During the same degree, Professor Nicolò Cesa-Bianchi, who co-supervised my thesis alongside Professor Alfio Ferrara, guided me through the statistical and algorithmic foundations of machine learning, helping me understand the assumptions that make learning from data possible and the guarantees that can be established for generalisation beyond observed examples. Information theory enriched this perspective through entropy, coding and the limits of transmission; with him I also studied computability and the Turing machine as part of a broader exploration of the foundations of computation.

This meeting of physics, statistics and learning underlies my interest in classical and quantum information theory, where I encounter questions about how knowledge is represented, how uncertainty is measured and which possibilities for processing and communication depend on the physical nature of systems. I continue to explore both the connections between these descriptions and the distinctive properties of quantum information.

### Interpretability and the humanities {#interpretabilita}

Meeting Professor Alfio Ferrara broadened this reflection towards the humanistic dimension of artificial intelligence, centring on the meaning of the knowledge produced by systems and the responsibility involved in their use. He gave me a decisive impetus to explore interpretability, because examining how a model reaches a conclusion allows us to discuss its reasons, recognise its limits and assess its relationship to the problem to which it is applied.

### Understanding and protecting systems {#sicurezza-sistemi}

During my postdoctoral research at the University of Milan, Professor Danilo Bruschi introduced me to the foundations of cybersecurity and systems architecture, teaching me, and continuing to teach me, how important it is to understand their operation in order to recognise the conditions that make them vulnerable.

I began studying program behaviour and execution traces, seeking to distinguish malicious activity, such as ransomware, through models whose reasoning could be examined. Security gave a practical direction to the interest in interpretability developed during my earlier studies, requiring analytical results to be connected with what actually happens within a system. During this period, I also participated in the MUSA project.

### Research and teaching {#didattica}

I teach Computer Science as an adjunct professor at the University of Milan, within Mathematics I and Information Technology for Geological Sciences, and Advanced Calculus and Stochastic Processes at Università Cattolica del Sacro Cuore; in the 2026/2027 academic year, I also contribute to Quantum and Post-Quantum Computing in the University of Milan’s Department of Computer Science.

In teaching, supporting courses, co-supervising theses and participating in Uni.ON, students’ questions lead me to revisit steps I tend to take for granted, seeking explanations that make both the ideas and the process of developing them understandable.

## personalInterests
### Forms take shape in the body {#geometria-vissuta}

In asanas, I encounter geometry in its material realisation, as a form takes shape through points of support, weight distribution and relationships between parts of the body. A line, an angle or a curve becomes something I can understand from within, feeling how a change in movement affects the balance of the whole. This possibility of constructing and inhabiting forms sustains my interest in yoga.

### My path through Vinyasa {#percorso-vinyasa}

Giada Merli introduced me to Vinyasa Krama, guiding my first steps in the practice. With Alessandra Martin, who has supported me and continues to do so with great patience and attentiveness, I have discovered the multiplicity of asanas over the years, through a learning process that has transformed my relationship with my body. Her teaching plays a central role in both Vinyasa and Yin Yoga, where the time spent in asanas allows me to notice sensations and their changes; with her, in 2026, I completed a 60-hour Teacher Training course on the yogic body and the sequencing of practices.

Camilla Vigorelli has enriched this path through Vinyasa by attending to the geometry and symbolic significance of asanas, helping me understand how constructing a figure can also express an image and a meaning. With her, studying directions, points of support and proportions becomes a way of entering more deeply into the form realised by the body.

### Ashtanga: discipline and learning {#disciplina-ashtanga}

With Alexandra Santos, I began studying Ashtanga Vinyasa, which I continue to practise, recognising an affinity between its discipline and studying mathematics: returning to exercises and devoting time to difficult steps gradually reveals relationships that initially escape understanding. Her guidance has taught me the value of this constancy, through a practice in which asanas, breathing and the direction of the gaze require continuous attention, developed together with movement.

Repeating the sequence recalls the thought of Heraclitus transmitted by Plutarch:

> ποταμῷ γὰρ οὐκ ἔστιν ἐμβῆναι δὶς τῷ αὐτῷ.
> For it is not possible to step twice into the same river.

The order of the asanas offers a stable reference, while the body and understanding change, making each return to practice an opportunity to recognise what exercise has transformed.

### Breathing and awareness {#pranayama}

With all my teachers, I have also explored prāṇāyāma practices, which have made me more aware of the countless intricacies of the human body: by attending to breathing, I began to distinguish variations and sensations that previously passed through experience without my recognising them.

### Holds, balance and paths {#appigli-equilibri}

In indoor climbing, which I learned with Luca Pani, the arrangement of holds invites me to find a possible movement between different points of contact, considering where to place my weight and how to prepare the next step. When hiking in the mountains, reading the terrain and finding a walking rhythm extend this relationship with space, through an attention that adapts to the characteristics of the route.

### Attention and interpretation {#attenzione-interpretazione}

Attending to emotions and expectations helps me distinguish what I feel from the stable image I tend to construct of myself, an exercise to which I attach the personal meaning of disidentification. Observing how expectations can shape perception also feeds my curiosity about judgement and interpretation; I regard these experiences as opportunities for personal reflection, distinct in nature from scientific verification, which rests on explicit hypotheses, shared procedures and independent evidence.
