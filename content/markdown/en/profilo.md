## intro
His main interests are cybersecurity, explainable artificial intelligence, quantum computing and quantitative finance. His background in computational physics, focusing on carbon nanostructures, informs his reflection on the relationship between models, observation and scientific explanation.

## biography
Marzio De Corato teaches Computer Science as an adjunct professor at the University of Milan and Advanced Calculus and Stochastic Processes at Università Cattolica del Sacro Cuore. His research, which began in the computational physics of nanostructures, has extended to statistics and explainable artificial intelligence, with particular attention to cybersecurity and the foundations of scientific knowledge.

He earned a bachelor's degree in Physics in 2009 and a master's degree with honours in 2012 at the University of Milano-Bicocca. In 2016 he completed a PhD in Physics and Nanosciences at the University of Modena and Reggio Emilia, with a thesis on the vibrational and optical properties of graphene nanostructures. His subsequent master's degree in Data Science and Economics, awarded with honours by the University of Milan in 2022, allowed him to explore epistemological questions in data analysis by bringing together methods from statistics, computer science and physics.

His early work addressed the physical properties of carbon-based systems, including schwarzites, graphene nanoflakes and graphene nanoribbons. Studying their structure and their electronic, optical and vibrational properties involved topological modelling and ab initio calculations. This period included research at CNR–NANO between 2012 and 2013 and contributions to journals such as Nano Letters, the Journal of the American Chemical Society and Nanoscale.

From time-series analysis and the evaluation of statistical forecasts, he moved to the study of traces produced by program execution, addressing malware and ransomware detection through hardware counters, sample entropy and probabilistic graphical models. In explainable AI, he examines, among other questions, how observed variables and data representations shape the reasons attributed to a classifier's decisions. His contribution presented at ANNPR 2026 addresses shortcut learning and inductive biases in the multi-view classification of behavioural traces.

His interest in the foundations of data analysis also informs his work on explainable AI, where the distinction between predictive performance and explanatory value is central. To assess what can actually be known through a model, he regards explicit assumptions, examination of the available data and the possibility of independently testing interpretations as essential. He addressed this relationship in an invited talk at IFCS 2026, focusing on the tensions between explainability and predictive performance.

At the “Giovanni Degli Antoni” Department of Computer Science of the University of Milan, he held a research fellowship from November 2022 to 2025 and a research appointment from April 2025 to March 2026; he also represented the department's postdoctoral researchers from September 2023 to 2025. His professional experience includes time-series analysis of COVID-19 and climate data, the latter through the Data Science 4Citizens project, as well as work in optoelectronics at Hamamatsu Photonics Italia from 2016 to 2019.

At the University of Milan, he teaches Computer Science within Mathematics I and Information Technology for Geological Sciences; at Università Cattolica del Sacro Cuore, he teaches Advanced Calculus and Stochastic Processes. In the 2026/2027 academic year, he also contributes to the teaching of Quantum and Post-Quantum Computing in the University of Milan's Department of Computer Science. He previously supported courses in statistics, mathematics, mathematical methods for finance, General Physics and Quantum Mechanics, participated in the Uni.ON educational project and co-supervised theses on cybersecurity, explainable AI and language analysis.

Alongside his work on nanostructure physics, his publications include the ransomware detection study published in Applied Stochastic Models in Business and Industry in 2025 and contributions on model interpretability. He also contributed to La Cybersecurity negli enti locali (2024) and L’Intelligenza Artificiale negli enti locali (2025), published by Milano University Press, and to the activities of MUSA — Multilayered Urban Sustainability Action.

## personalInterests
He began practising yoga with Giada Merli, who introduced him to Vinyasa Krama. Alessandra Martin subsequently played, and continues to play, a decisive role in his training; with her, in 2026, he also completed a 60-hour Teacher Training course on the yogic body and the sequencing of practices.

He began studying Ashtanga Vinyasa with Alexandra Santos and continues to do so today. He values the precision and attention with which she introduced him to the beauty of a discipline grounded in repeated sequences, breathing and concentration. He also continues his Vinyasa training through Camilla Vigorelli's classes; alongside these practices, he pursues Yin Yoga, in which longer-held postures offer a different experience of time and sensation.

Yoga offers an opportunity to observe the emotions that practice evokes and to rediscover, through bodily movement, the beauty of the forms and geometric relationships encountered during his university studies. A difficult posture can provoke apprehension, impatience or frustration; a movement that becomes familiar can bring confidence and satisfaction. Through breathing and returning to the same sequences, he seeks to recognise these changing states without identifying with them. Disidentification, in the sense he gives to this work, is the exercise of distinguishing the emotions that pass through experience from the stable self-image one tends to construct.

He learned indoor climbing with Luca Pani. Choosing holds, distributing weight and assessing a move require intention to be aligned with the actual possibilities of movement. He also enjoys hiking in the mountains, where a route is shaped by the terrain, environmental conditions and the pace of walking.

Encountering his own expectations, both in sequences on the mat and before a climbing wall, nourishes his interest in observation and interpretation. The question of how far judgement depends on what appears and how far on the patterns through which it is anticipated has a reflective counterpart in the epistemological questions encountered during his scientific training. Personal practice, in which this question concerns lived experience, remains distinct from scientific enquiry, which requires explicit hypotheses and evidence that can be assessed by others.
