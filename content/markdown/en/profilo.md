## intro
My work has developed from computational physics towards data analysis and explainable artificial intelligence, through a path in which nanostructures, traces of program execution and teaching have led me to examine the conditions that make a model useful for understanding a phenomenon.

## biography
I teach Computer Science as an adjunct professor at the University of Milan, within Mathematics I and Information Technology for Geological Sciences, and Advanced Calculus and Stochastic Processes at Università Cattolica del Sacro Cuore, while in the 2026/2027 academic year I also contribute to Quantum and Post-Quantum Computing in the University of Milan’s Department of Computer Science.

My education began at the University of Milano-Bicocca, where I earned a bachelor’s degree in Physics in 2009 and a master’s degree with honours in 2012, and continued at the University of Modena and Reggio Emilia, where I completed a PhD in Physics and Nanosciences in 2016 with a thesis on the vibrational and optical properties of graphene nanostructures; I subsequently earned a master’s degree with honours in Data Science and Economics at the University of Milan in 2022.

My early work, which included research at CNR–NANO between 2012 and 2013, addressed graphene nanoribbons and nanoflakes, schwarzites and other carbon-based systems, using ab initio calculations and topological descriptions to investigate how bond configurations affect electronic, optical and vibrational properties; contributions from this period appeared in Nano Letters, the Journal of the American Chemical Society and Nanoscale, among other journals.

After working in optoelectronics at Hamamatsu Photonics Italia from 2016 to 2019, I studied time series relating to COVID-19 and, through Data Science 4Citizens, climate data, encountering questions about forecasting and model evaluation that accompanied my subsequent work on traces generated by program execution.

In cybersecurity research, I analyse hardware counters, library calls and sample entropy to describe software behaviour that can be examined through probabilistic graphical models; in the study of classifiers, the choice of variables and data representations raises questions about whether explanations reflect the observed phenomenon or regularities introduced by data collection and encoding.

The relationship between predictive performance and explanatory value, which I have also explored through the epistemological foundations of data analysis, was the subject of my invited talk at IFCS 2026, while my contribution to ANNPR 2026 addresses shortcut learning and inductive biases in the multi-view classification of behavioural traces; this work also includes the ransomware detection study published in Applied Stochastic Models in Business and Industry in 2025.

At the “Giovanni Degli Antoni” Department of Computer Science of the University of Milan, I held a research fellowship from November 2022 to 2025 and a research appointment from April 2025 to March 2026, and represented postdoctoral researchers from September 2023 to 2025; my collaborations include MUSA — Multilayered Urban Sustainability Action and contributions to La Cybersecurity negli enti locali and L’Intelligenza Artificiale negli enti locali, published by Milano University Press in 2024 and 2025 respectively.

Alongside my teaching appointments, I have supported courses in statistics, mathematics, mathematical methods for finance, General Physics and Quantum Mechanics, participated in the Uni.ON educational project and co-supervised theses on cybersecurity, explainable AI and language analysis, through which students’ questions and problems also inform my research.

## personalInterests
As I repeat a yoga sequence, I observe how breathing, the distribution of weight and attention change the experience of a posture that retains a recognisable form yet may evoke fear, impatience, confidence or satisfaction, making the distance between what I expect from my body and what happens during practice perceptible.

I began with Giada Merli, who introduced me to Vinyasa Krama, and continued along a path in which Alessandra Martin has played, and continues to play, a decisive role; with her, in 2026, I completed a 60-hour Teacher Training course on the yogic body and the sequencing of practices, exploring the relationship between the organisation of movement and bodily experience.

I began studying Ashtanga Vinyasa with Alexandra Santos and continue to practise it, valuing the precision with which she introduced me to a discipline grounded in breathing and repeated sequences, while I continue my Vinyasa training through Camilla Vigorelli’s classes; alongside these experiences, I practise Yin Yoga, in which longer-held postures allow more time to observe changing sensations.

Indoor climbing, which I learned with Luca Pani, involves choosing holds and distributing weight while assessing a move, so that an imagined gesture must find its measure in the body’s actual possibilities; when hiking in the mountains, a related encounter unfolds through the terrain, environmental conditions and the pace of walking.

In paying attention to emotions and expectations, I find the personal meaning of disidentification, understood as the practice of distinguishing states that pass through experience from the stable image one tends to construct of oneself, while the forms encountered in yoga and climbing renew my interest in geometry, observation and interpretation.

This encounter accompanies my epistemological reflection because it makes the role of expectations in judgement tangible, while belonging to a personal experience whose meaning remains distinct from scientific verification, with its requirement for explicit hypotheses, shared procedures and independent evidence.
